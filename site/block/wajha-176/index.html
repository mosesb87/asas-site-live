When to use Musharrat

Most heroes make one promise. Musharrat makes one promise and immediately shows two ways to keep it. It suits a studio, agency or shop with two headline services that carry equal weight, where a single image would force a choice the business hasn't made.

The headline stays short because the panels do the explaining. In the capture, “Space that makes sense” sits above two plates captioned Website design and E-mail hosting, each with two lines of support. Replace the plates with a real photograph of each service and the block starts working without any other change.

What to watch

  • Keep the captions parallel. Two nouns of similar length. If one caption runs to a second line on tablet, the panels stop reading as a pair.
  • Two doors, no third. Each panel carries one small arrow button, and that is the whole invitation. Resist adding a headline button above them; if the page needs a single primary action, it belongs in the block below (an Ataba call to action).
  • Phone. The panels stack vertically. The second plate lands below the fold, so the caption of the first must be strong enough on its own.

Colour it

In grey, Musharrat reads as two equal weights. A kit changes that: the Blueprint kit tints the plates and leaves the type in ink; Clay warms the plates and makes the captions the loudest thing on screen. Try both in the Studio before you commit — the structure doesn't move.

Import into Elementor

  1. Open the block in the Studio, add it to a stack (or download it alone) and choose Download.
  2. In WordPress go to Templates → Saved Templates → Import Templates and choose the .json file.
  3. Open a page in Elementor, click the folder icon, choose My Templates, and insert “Musharrat”.
  4. Swap the two plates for your photographs. Everything else is already set.
