Why grey

Grey is a decision, not a placeholder.

A styled template makes you judge the colour. A grey one makes you judge the page: does the hierarchy hold, does the proof sit next to the claim, does the call to action arrive when the reader is ready? Settle that first and every brand you apply afterwards works harder.

How it works

Four moves. One page.

This is the order a page gets built in. Scroll, and the page on the right builds with you.

01 — Browse

Filter by what a block does, not what it looks like.

Search 3,881 blocks by room (hero, pricing, FAQ), by behaviour (static, carousel, accordion, tabs, split) or by its ASAS name. Every card is a browser capture of the real template.

02 — Stack

Put the blocks in the order a visitor will read them.

Reorder, swap or drop a section while changing it costs nothing. A page has three zones: the entrance at the top, the close at the bottom, and the argument in between.

03 — Colour

Apply a kit and judge the page in its own colour.

Twelve kits set ink, paper, lines and accent across every block at once. Then bring your own type, imagery, illustration and motion. The structure never moves.

04 — Export

Download one native Elementor file.

The same JSON format Elementor uses for its own library. Import it and every container, widget, spacing value and responsive rule is there to edit. Nothing is published for you.

Colour kits

Twelve kits. Try one on this page.

A kit is ink, paper, lines and one accent, applied to every block at once. This website runs on the same kits: pick one and watch the buttons, marks and accents repaint while the structure stays put. That is the whole idea.

Naming

Every block has a name, not a number.

An ASAS name is one Arabic word for the block's one move, with the English beside it. Names are easier to remember than “Hero 118”, and you can search by either.
