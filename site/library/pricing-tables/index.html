Choosing one

A pricing block has one job: make the right plan obvious.

Baqat · Murakkab

How to choose pricing tables

Most pricing tables fail the same way: every plan is shouted equally and the visitor has to do the comparing. The right block depends on how many decisions you are asking for.

Two plans — when the choice is really “starter or serious”. A two-column table reads in one glance and leaves room for a sentence on who each plan is for.

Three plans with a middle pick — when you have a clear recommendation. The raised or outlined centre card is the most tested pattern in pricing for a reason: it tells the visitor where to look first.

A comparison layout — when buyers compare features line by line — software, hosting, memberships. Rows of ticks beat paragraphs once the list passes six items.

What is inside the block

A Baqat block is an Elementor container holding one inner container per plan: a plan name, a price heading, a short line of support, an icon list of what is included, and one button. The featured plan carries its own border or background so a kit can colour it without you touching the others.

Import into Elementor

  1. Download the block (or your whole stacked page) from the Studio as a .json file.
  2. In WordPress go to Templates → Saved Templates and click Import Templates.
  3. Choose the file. It appears under Saved Templates with its ASAS name.
  4. Open any page in Elementor, click the folder icon, choose My Templates, and insert it.

Questions

Pricing tables, answered.

Do the pricing tables include a monthly/yearly toggle?

Some do. The toggle is built from Elementor’s own tabs, so it works without an add-on; the plain tables use static price headings you edit directly.

Can I change the number of plans?

Yes. Each plan is its own container. Duplicate one to add a plan or delete one to remove it; the row re-spaces itself.

Are these templates free?

Yes. Browsing, stacking and colouring need no account. Downloading the Elementor JSON needs a free account — a name, an email and a password, no card.

Do I need Elementor Pro?

Most blocks use core Elementor widgets only. Where a block uses a Pro widget, the Studio names it before you download, because Elementor silently drops widgets it doesn’t have on import.

How do I import a block into Elementor?

Download the .json file, open Templates → Saved Templates in WordPress, click Import Templates, choose the file, then insert it on any page from the editor’s template library.
